NCA 6.10 · Reference

Exam Day One-Pager

Logistics, scoring, and in-room tactics for the Nutanix Certified Associate 6.10 exam.

50 questions · 90 min $99 per attempt pass 3000 / 1000-6000 AOS 6.10 · PC pc2024.2 valid 3 years

Format

  • 50 questions in 90 minutes: multiple-choice and multiple-response. Multiple-response questions tell you how many to select.
  • Scaled scoring 1000-6000; pass at 3000. Questions are blueprint-weighted, so raw percentage is only a proxy.
  • Four sections: Lifecycle Management, Basic Administration, Environmental Health, Cluster Configuration.
  • Aimed at roughly 6-12 months of IT-infrastructure experience, ideally with Nutanix.

Register and schedule

  • Register through university.nutanix.com (free MyNutanix account). Cost is $99; discounts/vouchers appear periodically.
  • Delivered online proctored or at a Pearson VUE test center.
  • Recommended free prep courses: Nutanix Hybrid Cloud Fundamentals (NHCF) and Enterprise Cloud Administration (ECA).
  • The certification is valid for 3 years (Nutanix moved from 2 to 3 years on August 1, 2025).

Online proctoring checklist

  • Quiet, well-lit, private room; cleared desk, no notes, no second monitor, no phones.
  • Working webcam and microphone, stable internet.
  • Government-issued photo ID.
  • Run the system/network check ahead of time; proctoring software glitches are a known frustration, so schedule with margin.

In-room tactics

  • Flag and move on. If unsure, flag it; a later question often jogs the answer. Do not burn time early.
  • Read multiple-response counts. Select exactly the number asked.
  • Process of elimination. The NCA is not gotcha-heavy; eliminate the clearly wrong options first.
  • Watch the classic traps: HA (restart) vs Live Migration (no-downtime move); policy lives on the container not the pool; RF2 = 3 nodes, RF3 = 5 nodes; LCM does software AND firmware.

If you do not pass

  • Nutanix typically enforces a waiting period between attempts. Use it: drill the weakest section from your mock-exam breakdown rather than rushing back.
